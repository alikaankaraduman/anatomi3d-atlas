This file lists the third-party material in this repository and in the built app: who made it, its licence, what
was changed, and which files contain it. The built app ships the same information as `lisanslar.txt`, generated by

dialog shows it as well. If a source changes, `app/tools/licenses.mjs`, the About dialog in `app/index.html`, the
README and this file must be updated together.

The repository's own code, texts and questions have no licence yet (all rights reserved until the owner chooses

reservation: the adapted models below, each of which keeps the licence of its source, and the data derived from them

computed from the models' geometry), which stay under CC BY-SA 4.0 like the models they come from.

## 3D models (`app/public/models/`)

The `.glb` files are not the original files. They are **adaptations** of the sources below: converted, simplified,
renamed and grouped, and in some cases moved or reshaped. All of them are compressed with meshopt
(`EXT_meshopt_compression`) and quantised.

**Files:** `bones.glb`, `muscles.glb`, `nerves.glb`, `vessels.glb`, `organs.glb`, `lymph.glb`, `joints.glb`,
`attach.glb`, `attachm.glb`, `attachf.glb` (muscle attachment areas), `femalejoints.glb` (pelvic joints adapted to
the female pelvis); and inside `female.glb`, the sigmoid colon (with rectum and anal canal) and the ureters.

**Changes:** converted from FBX, simplified, renamed and regrouped. Latin names were corrected, and origin and
insertion areas that the source mislabelled were corrected from textbooks. In the female body, copies of the pelvic
joints, the sigmoid colon, the rectum and the ureters were reshaped to fit the female pelvis and bladder.

- Kristen Browne, NIAID 3D Printing and Biovisualization; Visible Human Female, U.S. National Library of Medicine.

### Drawn for this project

`urethra.glb` (female urethra) and `bartholin.glb` (Bartholin glands) are not in any source. They are schematic

## Data (`app/public/data/`)

- `meta.json` (names and groups): the English names and the group tree come from Z-Anatomy (CC BY-SA 4.0). Latin
  names follow Terminologia Anatomica 2 (from the Z-Anatomy term table), with abbreviations, accents and spelling errors
  corrected for this project. German and Turkish names were translated for this project; Wikidata labels
  ([CC0](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/)) served as hints for German.
- `holes.json` (cap triangles for filled sections) and the label and route points in the study-mode files are computed
  from the adapted models' geometry. Like the English names and the group tree in `meta.json`, they are adaptations
  of CC BY-SA material and stay under CC BY-SA 4.0 (the parts derived from the non-commercial models above keep those
  models' terms).
- The cortex parts are parcels of the Destrieux atlas (2009). Their names were checked against position and
  neighbourhood, and nine were corrected.
- `aanki.json`: topic titles and structure follow the Aanki wiki and the "Aanki Arbeitsübersicht" sheet (Fachschaft
  Medizin Aachen). The deck's cards are not included. This is an unofficial mapping: the project has no connection with
  Aanki, Fachschaft Medizin Aachen or RWTH Aachen, and they have not endorsed it.
- The study texts, muscle cards, questions and topic mappings (`brain.json`, `conduction.json`, `locomotor.json`,
  `muscles.json`, `sections.json`, `situs.json`, `thorax.json`, `uro.json`, `head.json`, `vessels.json`, `lymph.json`,
  `aanki.json`) were written for this project with AI assistance (see the README, "How this was built").
